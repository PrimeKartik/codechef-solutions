## 📚 Key Learnings

**1. Easy Speaking**
* Counting contiguous elements satisfying a condition doesn't require tracking the previous state; a single running counter that increments on success and immediately resets to zero on failure handles the logic in a pure $O(N)$ pass.

**2. Beginnings and Endings**
* When moving two elements to opposite ends of an array, their combined distance `(idx_first) + (N - 1 - idx_last)` can mathematically never equal or exceed `N - 1` unless they are the exact same element. This algebraic bound naturally filters out invalid states (like finding only one occurrence of a target) without needing explicit checks.

**3. Magic Mirror**
* Complex spatial transformations, such as coloring $X_1 - d$ and $X_2 + d$, often hide a simple mathematical invariant: $(X_1 - d) + (X_2 + d) = X_1 + X_2$. This means symmetric pairs must always sum to the exact same constant, reducing a heavy simulation into a simple two-pointer sum check.
