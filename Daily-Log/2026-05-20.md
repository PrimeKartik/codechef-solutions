## 📚 Key Learnings

**1. Three Powers of Two**
* Every `1` in a binary string represents a distinct power of 2. If a number has 3 or fewer `1`s, you can always split a larger power into two smaller ones ($2^x = 2^{x-1} + 2^{x-1}$) to get exactly three terms, with the only exceptions being $1$ and $2$ (which are too small to split into non-negative powers).

**2. Palindromic Prime Numbers**
* A fundamental rule of number theory: **Every palindrome with an even number of digits is a multiple of 11**. Therefore, `11` is the *only* even-length palindromic prime in existence. Once $N \ge 5$, the answer is permanently fixed at $1$ even-length prime.

**3. Easy Subarray Sum**
* To maximize a subarray sum with free zeroing operations, the absolute ceiling is the sum of all positive numbers. The minimum operations to achieve this is simply counting the negative numbers trapped *between* the first and last positive elements in the array.
